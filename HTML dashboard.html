Executive Summary: What Management Needs to Know

Even Condition Volume

Patient volume is evenly balanced across six primary conditions (~9,100–9,200 cases each), led slightly by Arthritis (9,218) and Diabetes (9,216).

Cost Consistency Over Time

Monthly average treatment costs stayed highly stable (~$24.9k–$26.3k) across 61 months without inflationary spikes or major seasonality.

Age-Independent Billing

Senior patients (60+) average $25,563 per visit—virtually identical to the network-wide average of $25,594. Billing is standardized regardless of age.

Operational Uniformity

Admission types (Elective, Urgent, Emergency) and insurance providers display uniform market share, signaling flat pricing structures across payers.

Key Business Analytics & Findings

Business Question 1

Network Patient Volume by Condition

Arthritis Highest

Finding: Arthritis impacts the highest number of patients (9,218), closely followed by Diabetes (9,216), Hypertension (9,151), and Obesity (9,146).

Business Implication: High-volume chronic management resources must be distributed evenly rather than over-specializing in one disease group.

Business Question 2

Patient Demographics & Age Distribution

Uniform Spread

Finding: Patient age spans uniformly across all brackets from age 13 to 89, with an average age of 51.5 years across 54,966 visits.

Business Implication: Facilities require versatile care capabilities spanning young adult wellness to comprehensive geriatric health services.

Business Question 3

Average Treatment Cost Trend Over Time (2019 - 2024)

61-Month Stability

Finding: Monthly average billing remains remarkably consistent between $24,915 and $26,324 over 61 months (Mean: $25,594.54).

Business Implication: Revenue predictability is high; however, management should examine fee structure responsiveness to broader health sector inflation.

Business Question 4

Senior (60+) vs Network Average Billing

Cost Parity

Finding: Senior patients (60+) average $25,563.59 per billing, showing virtually zero deviation from the overall average ($25,594.54).

Business Implication: Advanced age does not drive higher direct billing in this network, pointing to fixed procedure-based pricing algorithms.

Business Question 5

Admission Type Breakdown & Revenue Impact

Balanced Admission Types

Finding: Patient admissions are distributed almost equally across Elective, Urgent, and Emergency channels with near-identical average billing.

Business Implication: Bed capacity management and ER staffing can be planned with high predictability across all three triage tiers.

Ad-Hoc Strategic Analyses

Ad-Hoc Analysis 1

Insurance Provider Revenue Share

Insight: All 5 major payers (Cigna, Medicare, Blue Cross, Aetna, UnitedHealthcare) maintain an equal ~20% revenue share.

Executive Takeaway: Low payer concentration risk. Payer contracts yield standardized reimbursement rates network-wide.

Ad-Hoc Analysis 2

Diagnostic Test Results Quality

Insight: Diagnostic results show a balanced distribution across Normal, Abnormal, and Inconclusive findings.

Executive Takeaway: One-third of cases yield inconclusive results, indicating an opportunity to streamline follow-up diagnostics and lab efficiency.

Ad-Hoc Analysis 3

Length of Stay (LOS) vs Cost Correlation

Insight: Length of stay ranges from 1 to 30 days (Mean: 15.5 days), yet daily billing does not scale significantly with length of stay.

Executive Takeaway: Cost per day decreases with longer stays. Clinical discharge protocols should be optimized to reduce unbilled bed occupancy.

Executive Strategic Takeaways & Action Plan

Summary of enterprise risks, operational realities, and leadership directives

1. What is Happening?

The health network operates under exceptional operational uniformity. Patient volumes, billing metrics, insurance shares, and triage classifications display near-flat distributions across all hospitals and demographic segments.

2. Why Does It Matter?

While financial predictability and low payer dependence protect against revenue volatility, static average billing ($25.5k) across 5 years indicates pricing inelasticity that could erode operating margins during inflationary cycles.

3. Executive Priority Focus
  • Inconclusive Diagnostic Reduction: Audit diagnostic workflows to reduce the ~33.3% inconclusive test rate.
  • LOS Optimization: Implement discharge pathways for long-stay (>20 days) patients to increase bed throughput.
  • Strategic Pricing Review: Re-evaluate fixed fee schedules to better reflect complex medical conditions.
